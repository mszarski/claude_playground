# Status

What exists, how each part was checked, and what is open or known to be wrong. Numbers and
the commands that produce them are in `RESULTS.md`; reasons for choices in `DECISIONS.md`
(now to D-225); derivations in `PHYSICS_NOTES.md` (Secs. 12 to 33); keys in
`CONFIGURATION.md`.

Everything here is simulation and analysis. **Nothing has run on a vehicle, on the Popoto
boards, on a DGX Spark, a Jetson or any Sensor Bridge hardware. No sonar described here is
built.** Source levels, sensitivities, target strengths and the trial sites are design
values or placeholders, flagged `unconfirmed` in the files that hold them.

How things are verified, in the words used below:

- **CPU tests**: pytest and GoogleTest files in `tests/`, run in an x86_64 container with
  no GPU. A physics piece is tested against a closed form, a limit, an energy or
  reciprocity property, or an independent derivation.
- **GPU job**: the same suite and the benchmarks built with CUDA on a rented machine
  (`tools/gpu_job.sh`; T4, L4, A10G, A100).
- **Against a reference**: Bellhop and KRAKEN run as separate programs; the NVIDIA
  ultrasound reference's own decoder and runtime; measured channel soundings (UACR,
  Watermark, SFI Smart Ocean); APL-UW TR 0702 section by section.

### Simulator

- **Defaults changed with the owner's approval** (D-83 to D-90, D-104, D-105): four
  outbound passes of the arrival-time solve; Gaussian Doppler spectrum and the facet decay
  time for time spreading; small-slope bottom roughness; the bubble-layer surface
  backscatter model in its band; rock by its measured Lambert value; a default Lambert
  parameter of -29 dB; a bottom must be described (no assumed reflection coefficient);
  bounces and arrivals kept by energy with a cap that warns. Verified by CPU tests per
  default; every shipped scenario states its bounce order or arrival count, so D-104 and
  D-105 moved no shipped record.
- **Defaults aligned to the read references on 2026-11-26** (the owner: "apply every alignment
  without asking"; `docs/references/CONFORMANCE.md`, D-208): grain-size input by the printed
  relations (D-209); the sediment fit with small-slope and the row's grain-size prior, sigma2 up to
  0.005 (D-210, D-211); rock elastic in the simulator, Bellhop and the live chain's depth, multipath,
  coverage and planner models, shear unconfirmed (D-214, D-215); `bubble_layer_surface` with
  `extinction: dahl_1997` and an attenuated facet term, every backscattering law made bistatic by the
  geometric mean (D-216; surface reverberation about 1 dB lower in the multipath scenes); time
  spreading with Dahl 1996's large-scale slope (D-217; `flank_sst_*` and `sst_asiaex` moved). Each
  old behaviour stays selectable by name. Verified by CPU tests per change and the full GPU suite on
  the merged tree (D-225).
- **The sediment tier** (D-212, D-213): `penetration: constant | fluid | rough`; `fluid` (transmission,
  attenuation, the evanescent field below the critical angle) is used by `buried_cylinder` and
  `buried_objects` since 2026-11-26 (the owner); `rough` (roughness-scattered sub-critical
  penetration, one frozen realisation) is opt-in, no scene uses it. Both engines

  named site rows (Gallaudet 2001; Jackson and Briggs 1992 ×3; Williams and Jackson 1998, its sigma2
  ours); the soft rows' `sigma2_range` [0.0008, 0.005]. Small-slope stays the default (the owner).

### Simulator

26. **Range-dependent water is rings about the sonar** (D-110): a front across a map and
    horizontal refraction are not modelled. The rough-surface loss of eigenrays is not
    valid there. Bellhop's ray step is automatic; inside a sharp front in deep water a
    delay error of up to 30 microseconds was seen with 60 m steps (0.6 microseconds by the
    same formula for the shipped scenarios), its cause inferred, not read from Bellhop.
27. **The decay-time rule of time spreading is not settled by any data set.** It is worth
    0.1 to 0.4 dB in detection with the long-range array, less than the spread between
    seeds. The facet tail still does not match both fitting windows of the one clean
    measured bounce (D-107). With the large-scale slope (default since D-217) the SPACE'08
    steep-angle residual is measured over model 1.89 above 12° (1.28 with the Cox-Munk total), 1.68
    overall, 0.96 below; the opt-in exact Kirchhoff tail makes it 2.10 (D-218: the hypothesis that the
    tail's shape is the cause is rejected). Dahl 1999's measured spreads imply about half the slope law
    (a strict xfail). The cause of the steep residual is open.
28. **The coherent-phase wander is linear within a ping**, `legs: 1` is not verified
    through the kernel, and the long-range table is computed from the phase law, not
    through the kernel. Do not plan on coherent gain over several pings by surface paths
    at short range: 2 to 4 dB are lost in a 25 cm swell or sea state 4.
29. **`channel_replay.model_channel` keeps every ray at its first snapshot's delay**, so a
    change of delay reads as a frequency-flat phase there.
30. **The sediment fit tool fits with small-slope (D-210) and the `--sediment` row's grain-size
    prior (D-211, sigma2 1e-4 to 5e-3; `--no-prior-bounds` to turn it off).** Without the prior a
    small-slope fit can over-claim on one-frequency, volume-dominated data (5 of 10 silt draws flagged
    nothing); with it, w2 or gamma2 ends on a prior bound in 8 of 10 draws, and the tool says so ("set
    by the bound, not measured"). A small-slope fit takes 7.5 s against 0.2 s. `sediment_fit.fit` in the
    library takes no row and stays unbounded. The w2 widths of the prior are ours.
31. **The reverberation bindings still pass a placeholder sound speed** (`kSoundSpeedNotUsed`,
    1500 m/s, in `python/bindings/bind_simcpp.cpp`), and several study tools keep their own

## Discrepancies between the brief and the references

From reading the reference code (`HOLOSCAN_NOTES.md` section 1 has file and line for each):

- The reference's live imaging app is a native C++ loop around the non-operator
  `RoceReceiver`, not a Holoscan operator graph.
- The 1024-frame receive ring is the reference's own setting (`rx_pages`), settable from
  Python or C++ with no FPGA change. Stock `RoceReceiverOp` defaults to 2 pages.
- "Odd/even" mux: per the code, "odd" is elements 0–63 and "even" is 64–127. It is two
  contiguous halves, not an interleave. The mux is the TX73L64's own T/R switch select.
- Gain ramp: 12 dB and about 122 µs are the encoder's maxima, not the shipped settings
  (shipped: 12 dB, start 2 µs, ramp 10 µs). No AFE5932 attenuator register is written
  anywhere in the reference; the "unused 0–24 dB attenuator" was not found.
- A 150k-sample receive window exceeds two host-side caps (65,536 samples in the C++
  sequence compiler, 32,768 in the capture CLI). Neither is an FPGA limit;
  `Evm.init(samples=150000)` has no cap. Sustained 38.4 Gbit/s for 3 ms through the
  FPGA FIFO is untested by the reference, which streams about 60 µs per ping.
- No FPGA timestamp or ping counter reaches the application in the reference; the
  Sensor Bridge metadata has PTP times but the reference drops them.
- The reference transmits 5, 6.25 or 8.33 MHz only. Nothing in it supports sonar carrier
  frequencies; stages 3–6 need their own transmit chain.
- Docs say probe "L12-5 D"; code says "L7-3".

## Scenes and scenarios

- Scene objects (`sim/scene.py`, `test_scene_objects.py`): points, polar grids, wires,
  seafloor patches (flat, sloped, rippled), plates, spheres, boxes, cylinders, rocks, pool
  walls, volume clutter, hulls, cables, boats (hull, keel, propeller, wake), sea-surface
  wave facets, debris.
  Surface points carry a normal and scatter by Lambert's law about it; spheres and boxes
  cast shadows (a sphere on the seafloor leaves the floor behind it more than 10 dB
  darker in the forward-looking image).
- Targets and specular shapes (`sim/targets.py`, `test_scene_targets.py`,
  `test_scene_sediment.py`): highlight targets with strength against aspect, and smooth
  rigid spheres, cylinders, plates and discs whose specular highlights (glints) are
  modelled by the Kirchhoff approximation (it warns below ka = 10). A `seafloor` item
  with `model: fluid_sediment` follows the bottom scattering model instead of Lambert's
  law, and a seafloor can take a `bathymetry`.
- Not modelled: specular echoes from the point-cloud objects (hulls, boxes, rocks and
  pool walls stay Lambert surfaces; only the shapes above glint), creeping waves and
  elastic resonances, reflections between shapes, diffraction into shadows, shadows of
  boundary-reflected paths (they are treated as direct), shadows cast by plates,
  occlusion of moving glint targets, rotated boxes, meshes. Seafloor relief does not
  shadow itself in the scene (it does in the scattering-function reverberation). On
  bounce paths a target or glint has the direct path's aspect.
- Sonar configs beyond the brief's nine: `subbottom`, `popoto_4x7` (the plan's wiring),
  `fls_750k` (the plan's Phase 3 mid-range step; every value a placeholder).

  specular echoes in any gallery scene (the shapes that glint are used only in the three
  new target scenarios).
- In `flank_patrol` the −3 dB small vehicle is not detected (D-198: below the scene's bottom
  reverberation, 3.8 to 5.1 dB over its background against the gallery's 12 dB cut; left as a hard
  target on purpose, the owner's choice; the noise-only link budget's ~30 dB for it; with D-221's reverberation term 9.7 dB is
  predicted against 6.6 dB measured on the target's beam, `sonar-w2w3`), and 5 confirmed tracks are
  made for 2 objects: 1 mover (the submarine, 1.0 m from its outline since D-178's
  leading-edge range, was 25.8 m), 2 static (one the wreck, 3.5 m off), 2 uncertain.

## Motion estimation limits so far

- **Surge by along-track estimation (ASASIN ref. [9]: Brown, Gerg and Blanford, IEEE J. Oceanic
  Eng. 2019) is not implemented**: the paper is paywalled and no open copy could be opened. Surge
  comes from the DVL sample in each record.
- **Motion acceptance on eight seeds (D-164).** The bounds (lambda/20 sway, lambda/4 heave RMS;
  ours, milestone 8, ASASIN reports no motion accuracy) hold on seeds 1-8 at the default
  lambda1 = lambda2 = 1. Seed 2's miss (0.87 mm against 0.375 mm) came from holding ping 0's height
  on one noisy DVL sample; that height is now an unknown with its own Eq. 7 term (worst sway 0.24 mm).
  `minefield_sas` sway rose 0.081 -> 0.139 lambda RMS with that fix; explained in D-171: its last
  delay window held no seabed. The window energy gate (`min_window_energy_db`, -20 dB) is now the
  default and both SAS scenes' seabed covers the receive window: full-size `minefield_sas` sway
  0.022 lambda, heave 0.044; `wreck_sas` sway 0.053, heave 0.044 (host, D-171; the regenerated
  gallery on an L4, job 6b05051fc97b8ed4fcd725d4, and its D-178 regeneration, job
  6b054768c97b8ed4fcd78015, give the same to the digits shown). The
  `minefield_sas` echo-synthesis row of the GPU benchmark (14,870 scatterers) is of the old draw
  and has not been rerun.
- Delay estimation has a C++ port (host and CUDA, `MotionOptions.delay_backend`), bit-close to
  NumPy, which stays the default and the reference. On an L4 the CUDA path is slower than the
  host path (22.6 against 5.2 ms for 1248 windows; per-pair uploads and double sums). The fine
  delay is the phase at the integer peak (ours); ASASIN takes it at the interpolated point.
- Attitude and lever arms are tested: 2/1/1 deg RMS roll/pitch/yaw plus lever arms keep the
  level-platform bounds (sway mean error 0.04 mm).
- Known bathymetry is tested on a 1-in-10 slope and an undulating seabed; the undulating case
  leaves a sway bias of -0.32 mm (bound 0.375 mm RMS) from the one footprint point per range
  window. The test uses the same vertical-beam DVL model as the estimator.
- The DVL altitude is referred to the seabed under each ping (`seafloor_z` or the bathymetry,
  D-148). A datum away from the true seabed shifts the whole heave track by its error and
  nothing else: with `seafloor_z: auto` on seed 6, a 0.59 mm common offset, demeaned heave
  0.20 mm. Before, the datum put a step between ping 0 and the rest; the old, better-looking
  auto numbers (sway 0.054, heave 0.122 mm) were chance.
- The scenario's ping spacing (0.24 m) leaves a quarter of the phase centres overlapping.
  At the full 0.32 m advance there is no overlap and DPC cannot work, as in real systems.
- Dense linear solver (no SuiteSparse): fine for hundreds of pings.

## Conditioning limits so far

- The band-pass is the band selection inside `to_baseband`; there is no separate filter
  stage for data that stays at passband.
- The Doppler bank picks one hypothesis per record (the brief allows "a small bank");
  per-range or per-beam selection is not done.
- The reference rig's receive-attenuator ramp is a named model (`gpusonar/hardware.py`,
  `receive.gain_ramp` in `l12_5d.yaml`): it reproduces the reference's register values
  and enforces its encoder limits (12 dB, start within 10.23 µs, at most 122.4 µs). It is
  over before the sonar window opens, so range gain there is digital (`apply_tvg`).

## Kernel limits so far

- The trajectory table holds the residual of the platform *position* only. Attitude
  within a ping is still the first-order model (exact for constant rates).
- An LFM pulse received by a platform with a closing speed shows range-Doppler coupling
  (about 1 mm and 7° for 0.17 m/s at 200 kHz with a 2 ms, 40 kHz sweep). Nothing
  compensates this yet; the Doppler bank in milestone 6 is where it belongs.

- The sound-speed-profile option (eigenray travel-time table) is not in the kernel;
  delays are straight-ray at one sound speed per ping (plus the flat-interface Snell
  option). The simulator can bend rays (Bellhop); the imaging side cannot yet.
- `make_inputs` copies every ping's samples into one host buffer. A session
  (`BackprojectSession`) does that once and keeps the device copy between calls; a
  one-shot `backproject` call still does it every time.
- Real passband input is rejected; convert with `to_baseband` first.
- The FLS scan sequence for the reference rig and multi-angle compounding on our own
  probes are not done. Plane-wave
  compounding itself works (PICMUS).

## Simulator limits so far

- Everything is synthesised from point scatterers (see "Scenes and scenarios" for what
  that leaves out).
- Tiers: `free_field`, `image_source` (opt-in a tilted bed and walls as any plane, D-201; in C++
  too since D-205), `sediment` (`penetration: constant | fluid | rough`, D-212, D-213; the two
  buried scenes use `fluid`) and `eigenray` (Bellhop as an external program; without it those scenarios and
  tests skip), and the opt-in `eigenray3d` (Bellhop3D as an external program, 2026-11-26; only
  the illustrative `flank_seamount_3d`; no reverberation over relief at that tier;
  `docs/tasks/PROPAGATION_3D.md`). The shipped `eigenray` scenarios are N × 2-D: 3 to 4 dB too
  loud on bearings over a seamount's flanks against Bellhop3D (D-149, D-197); none of them has a
  bed that slopes across a bearing, and relief reverberation at that tier warns where one does.
- Sampled transmit pulses are synthesised by the C++ kernel on the host (D-81) and on
  the device (L4 benchmark: host-CUDA agreement 3.9e-7). With beams and absorption the
  sampled path is 3 times the analytic kernel (2.4 s against 0.80 s for 300 scatterers on
  128 channels); a large scene with a sampled pulse has not been timed. `table_factor: 0`
  and more than 32 `gain_knots` still run in NumPy, with a warning. CW cannot be given as
  a sampled pulse.
- `time_spread.doppler` runs on the C++ pairing (D-81). Path pairing has no CUDA backend.
- Still NumPy in a simulated ping, by cost: ambient noise (2.0 s of a 15–31 s ping; no
  C++ exists), the reverberation's random draws and K texture (NumPy on both engines by
  design, D-61), time spreading's taps, the scene modifiers, the ADC and board clocks.
  The full table is in `RESULTS.md`, "What in the simulator still runs in NumPy".
- One-way sources exist (`sources:`, D-123): pulses through the kernel, noise, tonals and
  files through a NumPy streamer on both engines (no C++ or CUDA version).
- The projector's direct sound, receiver overload and the basin's late tail are generated
  in NumPy and added after synthesis on both engines. The basin tail has no Doppler and
  none behind the direct sound or the boundary reverberation.
- Water that changes with range exists only at the `eigenray` tier, as rings about the
  sonar (D-110); the other tiers use the local speed and warn.
- The scattering-function reverberation assumes a flat boundary in a horizontally
  stratified medium, incoherent eigenray pairs and one Doppler shift for the whole pulse
  of each echo (no range walk), and draws new noise every ping. By default it is also monostatic (the
  transmitter is taken at the array centre and to move with it), shifts each azimuth by
  the receiver's horizontal speed as if every path were horizontal, ignores arrival
  elevation when steering to the hydrophones, takes the boundary as uniform and the
  statistics as Gaussian; transmit and element beam patterns are applied at each path's
  elevation at the sonar (D-37, D-43). `geometry: bistatic`, `strength_map` and
  `statistics: {model: k}` lift the defaults in that sentence. Over a record whose
  reverberation falls by more than about 50 dB the default generator leaks the strong
  start into the far end; `band_taper: true` removes this (none of the four shipped
  scenarios measured is affected; `sst_pursuit` turns the taper on).
- With `geometry: bistatic`, two of the standing assumptions can also be lifted:
  `scatterer_motion` gives the scatterers a mean velocity and a Doppler spread (D-72),
  and `bathymetry` gives the boundary relief, with facet angles, shadowing and, with
  Bellhop, a run per bearing (D-73).
- Still missing or wrong in the reverberation:
  - In bistatic mode components are keyed by a bin of total Doppler shift, half the pulse
    resolution wide, at most `max_doppler_bins` (128), with the exact Doppler factor
    (D-80). Within a bin the shift is a mean (at most half a bin off), and the direction
    is a mean within an elevation bin. The cost is more sequences: about five times on
    the Pursuit ping. The default (monostatic) mode keeps its horizontal-path,
    first-order Doppler.
  - The first receive element's pattern stands for all (a warning fires if elements
    differ; no shipped sonar's do).
  - Over relief, legs with a bottom bounce before the scattering cell are left out by
    default (5 to 6 dB low beyond five water depths over a reflective level bed), or kept
    as flat-bottom images with an unbounded error. Shadowing uses straight rays. With
    Bellhop, transmitter and receiver must be over one ground point.
  - No wind-driven surface drift, orbital wave motion or Bragg phase speed; no attitude
    rates in the platform Doppler; the spread of platform Doppler inside one component is
    collapsed to its mean.
  - No ping-to-ping coherence: wrong for a still sonar over a still bottom.
  - The K texture is independent between pings and between neighbouring cells. Its
    `shape` has no default: there is no confirmed value for any seabed. Since D-196 the shape
    can be set per cell from a density of scatterers or patches (Abraham and Lyons 2002); no
    density is known for any seabed either.
  - Near-specular reverberation is not removed by default when time spreading is on: the
    removal exists, opt-in, on `geometry: bistatic` items only (`exclude_specular`, D-123;
    `test_reverb_specular.py`). There is no near-specular surface scattering model.
  - The default generator's nearest-bin ripple (−1.4 to +1.7 dB between neighbouring 1 ms
    bins, right on average) is left as it is.
  - Bistatic mode has been run with Bellhop on a flat bed only with transmitter and
    receiver at one point (`test_reverb_relief.py::test_bellhop_over_a_level_bed_by_bearing_equals_the_flat_run`);
    a truly bistatic geometry with Bellhop has not been exercised. Its cost grows
    with sectors × rings × eigenrays of each leg.
- Boundary models: against Gallaudet's (2001) printed values at 68 kHz and 3 m/s
  (D-193) the surface model `bubble_layer_surface` lies 3.4 and 13.0 dB above the data, so it is
  not a lower bound against data; the bottom model agrees within 0.2 and 3.9 dB, and that site's
  data cannot tell the roughness models apart. The bubble-loss law (`hall_novarini`) is an upper estimate above about
  10 kHz; the roughness interpolation and composite split are our own, not the published
  ones (the composite split of 0.5 now matches a published criterion; since D-223 the opt-in
  `composite` and `combined` carry Wagner's shadowing, and the Mourad-Jackson 1989 model is an
  opt-in); layered bottoms are not modelled; rock reflects elastically since D-214 with unconfirmed
  shear values (item 49). Twenty sediments are named with per-parameter provenance, as class
  defaults and not site values, and five named site rows (D-209, D-224) carry one site each; rock and
  pebbles carry a measured Lambert parameter (D-88, D-132). The recommended bubble-loss law
  (`apl_1994`) has no stated range of validity. Since D-216 `bubble_layer_surface` takes Dahl et
  al.'s (1997) extinction by default (−44.4 / −43.7 dB against Gallaudet's −51.3 / −41.5 at 68 kHz,
  3 m/s), and the backscattering laws are made bistatic by the geometric mean (item 46).
- Time spreading: the decay time defaults to the facet rule (D-85), with Dahl 1996's large-scale
  slope since D-217 (`slope_law: cox_munk_total` the old one), the exact Kirchhoff tail opt-in
  (`decay_time: kirchhoff_go`, D-218), and the Doppler spectrum to a Gaussian (D-84). Angle and frequency spreading use small-angle geometry,
  isotropic slopes and no shadowing. Against measured channels the Gaussian shape holds
  at three sites and the decay-time rule is not settled (open item 27).
- `fls_300k` needed an analogue gain ramp to register a 300 m echo in 16 bits; the ramp in
  its config is a placeholder, flagged unconfirmed.

## Known deviations from the paper

Each is argued in `DECISIONS.md`; the paper's own gaps and how they were filled are
D-01 to D-25, tied to `PAPER_NOTES.md` Part 2. Deviations in implemented code:

- Whitening and median TVG apply the square root of the paper's gain, read as a power
  gain (D-20); the literal reading is kept as an option and tested not to flatten.
- The motion solve uses ping positions as parameters instead of per-ping velocities; the
  minimiser is the same (D-30).
- The kernel tests each (point, ping) pair against the beam in float32 before the float64
  delay solve (D-35); images are unchanged.
- Two device slabs instead of one allocation per run: the resident collection and one per
  call (D-40).
- Not in the paper at all: the flank array stages, the long-range propagation and
  reverberation models, the capture sources.
