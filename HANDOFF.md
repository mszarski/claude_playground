# Handoff

This file is the task list and the memory for whoever continues hydropt
without the `bd` (beads) tool this repository used for task tracking.  It
holds, in full, every open task with its acceptance criteria, and every
stored lesson.  If you do have `bd`, the same content is in it (`bd prime`,
`bd list --status=open`); if you do not, work from here and **append your
status to each task below as you go** (what you did, what you measured,
what is left), so the next agent does not need `bd` either.

## Where things stand

Branch `claude/hydropt-acoustic-tracer-15kecr`.  The suite is green (578
tests, ~22 min on 4 cores).  Examples 01-30 pass at 120 kHz and 21-30 at
330 kHz (`HYDROPT_SONAR=330`); their figures are regenerated by a run
(gitignored).  The README is the account of the physics and of what each
piece cost; `CLAUDE.md` is the working guide (conventions, the rules that
were expensive to learn, how to add an example); `examples/LABELS.md` is
the labelled-data how-to.  Read `CLAUDE.md` and the "Lessons" section at
the end of this file before changing anything.

## Done since this file was written

### Fit the scene to a real picture  (`claude_playground-a8v`, priority 1, feature)

`hydropt/scenefit.py` and `examples/30_fit_scene_to_picture.py`: the
seabed's and the surface's strength, the head's tilt, a gain offset and
the ambient level fitted to a picture loaded from a file (`load_picture`,
`cartesian_to_polar`) on the swath's level against range (`range_profile`,
a sector median smoothed over 5 m), by Adam through the coherent picture
(the parameters scale amplitudes or steer the fan's weights, none moves a
scatterer, so the coherent gradient serves; the tilt needs no retrace).
Proven on a picture rendered with hidden values and independent seeds:
seabed to 0.8 dB, gain to 0.2 dB, tilt to 0.5 deg, surface to 0.7 dB at
120 kHz; the noise level is undetermined where the floor is under the
reverberation and is reported, not checked.  Four tests in
`tests/test_scenefit.py`.

**Status:** done at both heads.  What remains for a real ping: put it
through `load_picture` (an `.npz` of the polar image in dB with its
bearings and ranges, or a Cartesian image with its axes), hold the
altitude and water depth the navigation gives, run `fit_scene`, and then
fit the boat on the fitted background with 22's rules and a loss
restricted to the labelled region.

### Prototype CV detector trained on the generator, then improved through the differentiable renderer  (`claude_playground-2dd`, priority 1, feature)

Why: the README's 'Towards ML' items 3 and 4; the labels give the targets, the renderer gives gradients a detector cannot get from data.

What, in three steps, each its own script under ml/ with a README:
1. ml/detector.py: a small CenterNet-style CNN on the polar image [beams, bins] (log-compressed, normalised per range by the median as 21's display does): a centre heatmap per class (boat hull, buoy, kelp forest, school of fish, noise spoke), box size regression in (beams, bins) and an offset head; trained on the generator's output (labels' polar_box), held-out seeds for validation, both heads. Report AP per class at IoU 0.5 and the confusion between boat hull and buoy.
2. ml/adversarial.py: for the trained detector, take a generated scene where a hull is labelled visible, make the scene parameters learnable (hull diffuse_db and heading, the sea's wind through the display gain, the seabed strength) and descend the detector's confidence on that box through the renderer (PictureRenderer.picture is differentiable in the targets; the detector on the polar image). Add the found frames to training; report the AP change on held-out seeds and what the search found (which parameter moved most). Compare against random sampling of the same parameters at equal cost.
3. ml/rescore.py: score each detector proposal by the residual of 22's fit (incoherent model picture, gain frozen, loss restricted to the proposal's box dilated by a beam) from the box centre and the class's hull; show on generated frames with speckle false alarms that the residual separates true boxes from speckle better than the confidence does (ROC on both).

Acceptance: step 1 AP > 0.8 on hulls at 120 kHz held-out seeds; step 2 finds at least one confident miss that 100 random draws did not, and adding the adversarial frames does not lower held-out AP; step 3's residual ROC AUC exceeds the confidence's on the false-alarm set. All three run on CPU at small scale (the generator at 20 frames) and on GPU at full scale; runtimes printed.

**Status:** not started.  Depends on the generator above.

## Physics and engineering still owed

These are real defects and gaps, each measured; none blocks the four
pieces above.  In the order they matter for the pictures:

### Physical optics on a mesh is a plane wave per patch: near-field bodies under-read their specular  (`claude_playground-cva`, priority 2, bug)

mesh PO evaluates each patch at one incidence direction (the patch centroid's). A 4 m cylinder at 30 m (far field L^2/lambda = 1 km at 100 kHz) split in two 2 m patches has each centre 2.2 deg off the line of sight, outside the 0.2 deg lobe, so broadside reads -11 dB instead of +23. Examples 17/18 use one patch to keep the intended geometry; 21's 30 m hull at 250 m (far field 60 km) has the same issue in principle. Fix: spherical-wave PO with per-facet incidence and Fresnel phase.

**Status:** open.

### Reverberation's return leg is direct-only while the target's sums every image path  (`claude_playground-e9z`, priority 1, bug)

reverberation_arrivals returns each patch by ONE path -- the reverse of the outbound ray, 1/r^2 -- while target_arrivals with eigenrays on both legs composes every inbound path with every outbound path: 8 x 8 = 64 combinations per highlight in examples/21, against 8 (outbound, with its reciprocal return) for a reverberation patch. Physically a Lambert patch scatters into all directions and every image path back to the array collects some of it, so the reverberation is missing its return-leg multipath: up to +9 dB in a hard-bottomed 30 m waveguide at low grazing where |R| ~ 1. Symptom: restoring the target's bounce paths took the boat from +16.1 to +34.1 dB over the clutter, 18 dB, where a few dB was predicted; 10 log10(64) = 18.1 dB says the 64 combinations are each near full strength and pile onto a hull 71 range cells deep. Either both channels get return-leg images or neither; the asymmetry favours the target by the return-path count. This defect predates today and was in the old images too.

*Notes:* Re-sized with the receive stave in place (ESTIMATE, not measured): the stave's 21 deg response is on the -5 deg boresight, so of the return-leg images the reverberation is missing, the surface one (~3 deg up) is in-beam and the seabed ones (~10 deg down, 15 off boresight) sit on the skirt at ~-13 dB. So the asymmetry is worth about +3 dB on the clutter now rather than the +9 first estimated for point elements. Still real, still unmeasured; measuring it means implementing return-leg images in reverberation_arrivals.

**Status:** open.

### Occlusion culling perturbs a fine mesh's PO sum  (`claude_playground-5we`, priority 2, bug)

On the smooth boat_hull_mesh at 1100x192 facets (0.029 m), occlusion=True gives +13.1 dB at beam aspect and -17.5 at 70 deg where occlusion=False gives +14.5 and -8.3; at 1x and 2x resolution the two agree exactly. A convex hull has nothing to occlude, so the depth buffer is culling facets on a continuous surface at fine resolution -- the cell and tolerance default from the median edge length, and both shrink with the mesh until the margin is no longer a few bins of the surface's own sagitta. Small next to the other defects; measured, not fixed.

**Status:** open.

### Differentiable shadow edge needs a silhouette distance  (`claude_playground-d3n`, priority 2, feature)

segment_mesh_transmission returns a hard 0/1 mask, so the shadow edge carries no gradient - and the edge is exactly the observable that encodes a bottom object's height, so a height cannot be fitted through it. A per-facet blend does not work: silhouette facets are geometrically correlated and treating them as independent over-counts them (a ray 0.05 m outside a tessellated sphere of radius 2 came back 99.7% occluded with a 0.1 m blend). The right formulation is a signed transverse distance to the body's silhouette (the edges shared by a front and a back facet), blended once.

**Status:** open.

### Wake groups carry no intrinsic wave phase, so the near field piles up  (`claude_playground-62h`, priority 2, bug)

wake_elevation sums each group as cos(k . (x - centre)), which omits the group's own wave phase. Referencing the wave to its emission event gives an extra -omega*age/2 (since k.c_g*age = omega*age/2 in deep water), and without it every emission time is in phase at its own centre. Two consequences, both measured: the field piles up coherently within a few wavelengths of the vessel -- peak 8.4 at 10-30 m astern against 0.41 at 180 m on a straight 6 m/s track -- and that lump sets the height field's normalisation; and the trail decays about as 1/D instead of the classical 1/sqrt(D). Adding the term raises the whole field about 4x and flattens the decay, but it aliases: d(omega)/d(theta) = g sin/(V cos^2) is 6.5 rad/s per radian at 62 deg, so at age 100 s adjacent directions differ by 7 rad and the sum becomes noise. A correct fix needs the amplitude from the (emission time, direction) -> (x, y) Jacobian -- the same fan-density splat hydropt.launch already does -- which also gives the cusp caustic for free, rather than more directions.

**Status:** open.

### Image beams: fold a Gaussian beam at the waveguide boundaries  (`claude_playground-7ck`, priority 2, feature)

Beam summation is validated to 0.001-0.22 dB against the Pekeris modes where the beam fits inside the waveguide, and is +2.1 to +2.9 dB with a range trend where it does not (100 m channel at 200 Hz, Fresnel scale 69 m). The transverse Gaussian currently extends through both boundaries as if in free space. Tuning beta cannot fix it: the minimum width over beta is the Fresnel scale. The fix is to fold each beam at the surface and seabed via image beams, as a mode-like sum of reflected transverse profiles.

**Status:** open.

### Examples 15 and 20 need re-running after the hull flotation fix  (`claude_playground-d55`, priority 2, task)

The boat was placed at z=draught in examples 15, 20 and 21, which sinks the wetted hull by its own draught because boat_hull_mesh already has its waterline at z=0. At the 1 m draught those two examples use, the boat sat 1 m under. The code is fixed but their committed printed output and figures were produced with the old placement, so the boat's echo position, its extent at -10 dB and its signal-to-reverberation figures are all from a boat 1 m too deep. Re-run both and refresh the numbers in the README table if they move.

**Status:** open.

### beamform raises an opaque torch error on mixed-dtype inputs  (`claude_playground-u69`, priority 3, task)

beamform does not check that arrivals, element positions and freqs_khz share a dtype. Mixing them reaches torch.polar, which raises 'Expected object of scalar type Float but got scalar type Double for second argument' pointing at hydropt/beamform.py:311 and naming neither the offending argument nor the caller's mistake. This is a realistic user error: load a mesh or build an array at one precision and a scene at another. Check the dtypes at entry and raise naming the arguments that disagree.

**Status:** open.

### PRB-style backward pass  (`claude_playground-du6`, priority 3, task)

The README's "Autograd strategy" scopes it: a path-replay backward pass in
the manner of misuka, replacing the stored RK4 graph, if memory in a fit
ever binds.  It has not: a fit step is 2.6-4.2 s and the working set is
small (see "Where the time goes now").  Do this only when a measured fit
runs out of memory.

**Status:** open, not needed yet.

## Lessons (the stored memories, verbatim)

Each of these cost at least a day.  They are also folded into `CLAUDE.md`'s
"Rules that were expensive to learn"; this is the complete list with the
measurements.

* **beamform-shading-none-is-unit-sum.**  beamform(shading=None) uses UNIT-SUM uniform weights (shading_window(n,'uniform')), not ones: beam_power_scale must be of those weights or a calibrated picture reads 20log10(n) dB low (18 dB for 8 elements, found by test_sequence's absolute emission level check). A propeller's spoke comes and goes with aspect: hull mesh shadow blanks it bow-on (0 of 14 paths), propeller_directivity (20 dB bow, 10 abeam, 6 dB wake notch astern) does the rest.

* **display-gain-fixed-for-fitting.**  Fitting through a median-TVG display: freeze the gain from the measurement (examples/21 display_gain + display(gain=)) -- a median re-derived per trial image gives an analytic gradient 6x the finite difference (order statistic derivative is one beam's); fixed gain or mean agree to ~7%.

* **eigenray-solver-never-cap-bracketed-paths-by-miss.**  Eigenray solver: never cap bracketed paths by miss distance (it dropped the direct path); spreading Jacobian must be in the path's own launch/arrival frames, not the chord's tangent plane (bounce paths read +3 dB otherwise).

* **float32-coherent-picture.**  float32 is fine for pictures and for fitting on the incoherent (expected-intensity) picture; the coherent picture's loss scatters by 2e-5 between points 0.05 mm apart in float32 (more than it changes over 0.4 mm), so wavelength-scale gradient checks of the coherent picture need float64 (arrival time 0.33 s quantised to 40 ns).

* **float32-picture-run-to-run.**  21's speckle-sensitive numbers (raw contrast +35.8 vs +38.1 dB, 43630 vs 43629 swath cells) moved between sessions with NO code path change (verified: with and without the beam_tilts cache, three runs today agree exactly). Runs under identical conditions repeat bit for bit; runs under different load/thread scheduling differ in float32 reductions and the median-gain and multi-look metrics amplify that. Compare 21 against a run made alone in the same session, not against an old log.

* **fresnel-glint-limit.**  A hull's specular flash needs the Fresnel zone sqrt(lambda R) to be comparable to the PO patch: 1.8 m vs 5 m at 120 kHz/250 m works, 0.75 m at 330 kHz/125 m does not (plane-wave PO per patch, bead cva). Gate glint checks on it; a spoke lies on the emitter's bearing (the propeller), not the boat's.

* **labels-from-fields.**  Labels off the fields (hydropt/labels.py): a target's mask = its own power beats everything else by 3 dB AND within 35 dB of its own peak AND inside its geometry + 2 beams (polar_gate). Without the dynamic-range gate a +48 dB hull's -43 dB Hamming sidelobe ring beat the reverb and the box spanned the swath. add_receiver_noise(complex_output=True) gives the noisy field so |total|^2 equals the picture.

* **mesh-po-chunking-speed.**  MeshScattering: facet_chunk=256 + checkpoint (108 blocks per echo) costs 2.5x over one 4096-facet block per patch kept for backward (6 MB working set at ~200 direction pairs): 5.8 s -> 2.3 s per echo at 300 m. Library default stays conservative; examples 21-23 set facet_chunk=4096, checkpoint=False. float32 vs float64 is only ~12% of a fit step; the PO facet integral is the cost.

* **never-take-sqrt-of-a-beamformed-image-without.**  Never take sqrt of a beamformed image without a floor: an exactly-zero cell gives inf*0 = NaN in the gradient of every parameter.

* **occlusion-working-set.**  segment_mesh_transmission's block is segments x facets: bound it by the product (4M elements), not the facet count -- a 300 m wall in a 317k-patch picture at 2048 facets a block was 7.8 GB a temporary and the process was OOM-killed at 14 GB.

* **rx-pattern-called-per-highlight.**  target_arrivals calls rx_pattern/tx_pattern once per highlight: anything expensive inside them multiplies by the point count (21's receive_beam re-measured the beam width via beam_3db_deg, 70 ms a call: 1200 kelp points = 80 s an echo, fixed with lru_cache on beam_tilts_deg). Keep patterns cheap or cached.

* **schema_version.**  1

* **template-fit-incoherent-beamform.**  Fitting a target's position on a coherent picture: use beamform(coherent=False) for the model (expected intensity) against the coherent measurement. The coherent image is speckle in position (5 cm across-track at 250 m), a loss on it is a cusp on a +-2 m plateau whatever the blur. Finish the fit at ~0.5 m blur: at 2-4 m blur the direct/surface-ghost fringe (0.2 m apart, phase turning along the hull) biases the blurred minimum ~1.75 m in range.

## How the labels, sequences and examples fit together

* `examples/21_long_range_300m.py` is the base scene every later example
  imports (`_ex21()`); its "Construction and assumptions" block lists
  every constant and switch.
* `hydropt/sequence.py`: `PictureRenderer` (21's pipeline with the
  reverberation's beams formed once per pose), `Trajectory`,
  `sequence()` for a moving target (28), `ownship_sequence()` for a moving
  sonar (29), `relative_pose`, `reframe_height_field`.
* `hydropt/emission.py`: what a propeller radiates and its directivity
  (the spoke, 24 and 28).
* `hydropt/labels.py`: boxes, masks, centroids, classes per target from
  the fields (`picture(..., labels=True)`); `examples/LABELS.md` is the
  record format and the how-to.
* Adding an example: `CLAUDE.md`, "Adding an example"; copy 23 for a
  scenario, 28 or 29 for a sequence, 22 for a fit.  Run it alone (four
  cores: never two runs at once), read its PASS/FAIL lines and look at its
  figure before committing; library changes get the full suite.
